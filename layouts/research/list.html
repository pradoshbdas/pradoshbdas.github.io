Mapping the Milky Way’s Chemical Structure with Gaia RVS

Stellar chemical abundances preserve a record of the processes that have shaped the formation and evolution of the Milky Way. This study employed The Cannon, a data-driven spectral model, to derive stellar parameters and elemental abundances for approximately 800,000 Gaia DR3 RVS spectra, using high-resolution GALAH DR4 observations as the reference. Trained on approximately 14,500 stars common to both surveys, the model estimated effective temperature, surface gravity, projected rotational velocity, metallicity ([Fe/H]), and the abundance ratios [Ca/Fe], [Si/Fe], [Ti/Fe], and [Ni/Fe]. The resulting catalogue provides reliable stellar labels for more than 600,000 stars, with measurements validated against independent spectroscopic data and stellar clusters. Notably, this work represents one of the early studies to recover the characteristic chemical bimodality of the Galactic disc from Gaia RVS spectra using Ti as an α-element tracer, revealing the distinct high- and low-α sequences in the [Ti/Fe]–[Fe/H] plane. These results demonstrate the potential of medium-resolution Gaia spectroscopy for investigating the chemical structure and evolutionary history of the Milky Way.

Tracing Gaia–Sausage–Enceladus through Neutron-Capture Chemistry

The chemical signatures of accreted stellar populations provide valuable constraints on the assembly history of the Milky Way and the chemical evolution of its disrupted progenitors. Building on the GALAH–Gaia RVS analysis, this study extended The Cannon to derive neutron-capture abundances of zirconium (Zr), cerium (Ce), and neodymium (Nd) for approximately 360,000 red giants. The sensitivity to these elements was investigated through differential spectral analysis and cross-correlation techniques. Combining α-element and neutron-capture abundance ratios within a probabilistic chemical-classification framework, the analysis identified approximately 2,300 probable members of the ancient Gaia–Sausage–Enceladus (GSE) merger. The chemically selected population exhibits distinctive abundance patterns, particularly in [Ca/Ti], [Ti/Ce], and [Nd/Zr], relative to comparison halo stars, with independent orbital analysis providing further constraints on their association with GSE. These findings highlight the diagnostic potential of multi-element stellar chemistry for identifying accreted populations and reconstructing the chemical evolution of disrupted Galactic systems.

Tracing the Origins of Heavy Elements Across the Milky Way with the Gaia-ESO Survey

The Galactic enrichment of elements heavier than iron reflects the cumulative contributions of different stellar nucleosynthetic processes. Barium and europium, predominantly associated with slow (s)- and rapid (r)-process nucleosynthesis, provide complementary diagnostics of these enrichment pathways. This study employed The Cannon to analyse approximately 36,000 VLT/FLAMES spectra from the Gaia-ESO Survey, using a reference sample of approximately 800 stars to derive homogeneous stellar parameters and Ba and Eu abundances. Dedicated quality criteria and uncertainty estimates enabled the identification of approximately 21,000 reliable Ba measurements and 400 Eu measurements, including more than 10,000 newly determined Ba abundances and over 100 Eu abundances. Comparisons with independent spectroscopic surveys, benchmark stars, and stellar clusters support the reliability of the resulting catalogue. These measurements expand the observational basis for investigating neutron-capture enrichment across Galactic stellar populations and demonstrate the applicability of data-driven spectral modelling to chemical-abundance analyses of large spectroscopic datasets.
