01 / 03MNRAS · 2025

Mapping the Milky Way’s Chemical Structure with Gaia RVS

Stellar chemical abundances preserve a record of the processes that have shaped the formation and evolution of the Milky Way. This study employed The Cannon, a data-driven spectral model, to derive stellar parameters and elemental abundances for approximately 800,000 Gaia DR3 RVS spectra, using high-resolution GALAH DR4 observations as the reference. Trained on approximately 14,500 stars common to both surveys, the model estimated effective temperature, surface gravity, projected rotational velocity, metallicity ([Fe/H]), and the abundance ratios [Ca/Fe], [Si/Fe], [Ti/Fe], and [Ni/Fe]. The resulting catalogue provides reliable stellar labels for more than 600,000 stars, with measurements validated against independent spectroscopic data and stellar clusters. Notably, this work represents one of the early studies to recover the characteristic chemical bimodality of the Galactic disc from Gaia RVS spectra using Ti as an α-element tracer, revealing the distinct high- and low-α sequences in the [Ti/Fe]–[Fe/H] plane. These results demonstrate the potential of medium-resolution Gaia spectroscopy for investigating the chemical structure and evolutionary history of the Milky Way.

02 / 03MNRAS · 2026

Uncovering traces of an ancient galactic merger

Building on our previous GALAH–Gaia analysis, we extended The Cannon to infer neutron-capture abundances of Zr, Ce and Nd for 357,415 red giants observed by Gaia RVS. Using a training sample of 2,747 GALAH DR4 stars, we derived 11 stellar labels and investigated weak neutron-capture features through differential spectral analysis and cross-correlation. We then developed a probabilistic chemical-classification framework, combining logistic regression with MCMC sampling, to identify candidate members of the Gaia–Sausage–Enceladus accretion event. Independent kinematic constraints were used to assess the chemically selected populations.

03 / 03A&A · 2026

Tracing neutron-capture enrichment with the Gaia-ESO Survey

We applied The Cannon to 36,263 FLAMES/GIRAFFE HR15N spectra from the Gaia-ESO Survey to derive homogeneous stellar parameters and the neutron-capture abundances of barium and europium, which trace s- and r-process enrichment, respectively. Trained on 782 reference spectra from Gaia-ESO DR5, the model incorporates multi-dimensional quality flags and signal-to-noise-dependent uncertainties to assess abundance reliability. The resulting catalogue provides approximately 21,000 reliable Ba measurements and 400 Eu measurements, with validation against Gaia Benchmark Stars, GALAH DR4 and open clusters. These measurements offer new constraints on neutron-capture enrichment across Galactic disc populations and a framework for future spectroscopic surveys.
